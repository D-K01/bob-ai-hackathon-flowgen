# Problem Statement

## Background

Urban water utilities operate large, distributed networks of pipelines, valves, reservoirs, and pumping stations. In the FlowGen target scenario, Pune–PCMC water operators receive continuous readings from pressure, flow, acoustic, chlorine, pH, and turbidity sensors across multiple zones.
## The Problem

Underground leaks and potential water-quality incidents are difficult to detect early because related warning signals appear across separate sensor streams. Operators must manually connect pressure drops, flow imbalance, acoustic anomalies, and water-quality changes, delaying identification of the probable pipe segment and the safest isolation actio

## Who is Affected

The primary users are municipal water-control-room operators, field maintenance supervisors, and emergency-response teams responsible for maintaining safe water supply across Pune–PCMC zones. Residents and businesses in downstream neighbourhoods are affected when an incident is detected late or the response isolates more of the network than necessary
## Why It Matters

A delayed response can waste treated water, damage roads and property, interrupt supply, and increase potential public-health risk. During a suspected contamination event, operators need to quickly identify which areas may be affected, isolate the smallest safe network section, dispatch crews, and communicate clearly with residents

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Existing systems often display sensor readings, alarms, GIS maps, and operational procedures separately. They alert operators about individual abnormal values but do not automatically correlate multiple signals, estimate the probable affected pipe zone, simulate downstream impact, or provide an explainable prioritized response plan
